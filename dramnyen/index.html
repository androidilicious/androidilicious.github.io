About the dramnyen

The dramnyen (also dranyen, dramyin) is a long-necked, fretless lute played across the Himalaya. It accompanies Bhutanese zhungdra and boedra songs and the dramnyen cham dance.

Strings

The Bhutanese form has seven strings in three courses. This model uses one commonly cited tuning:

  • g G: a pair one octave apart
  • c′ c c: two unison strings plus the short string
  • f f: a unison pair

The short string stops halfway up the neck and has its own peg in the side of the neck. It sounds an octave above its two neighbours. Stop the middle course on the upper half of the neck and it keeps ringing open.

Build

  • Body carved hollow, with a skin soundboard on the lower part
  • Rosette sound holes in the wooden shoulders
  • No frets: pitch depends on where the finger lands
  • Pegbox carved as a chusin (sea monster) head

Playing here

  • Finger pad: each lane is one course, open string on the left
  • On the model: pluck over the body for an open string, press on the neck for a stopped note
  • Drag along one course to slide
  • Keyboard: rows Q, A and Z play the f, c and g courses. Space strums
  • Hand tracking: your camera follows both hands. It needs camera access, so it runs in the downloaded file, not inside Claude

The sound is synthesized (a plucked-string model), not a recording, and the carving and painting are stylised. Tuning and construction follow Wikipedia: Dramyin.
